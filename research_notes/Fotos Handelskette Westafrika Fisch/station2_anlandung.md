## Which photos show the port of Nouadhibou or Dakar fish landing/processing?

### Takeaway
The best free-licensed option for the landing scene is the Wikimedia Commons photo "Nouadhibou,PortArtisanal1.jpg" (CC BY-SA 3.0, by Bertramz, 2010). The FAO Digital Media Hub has a 2024 series from Nouadhibou: the port, ships, and a fish-processing factory producing frozen fish. That series is a good source for "cold storage / freezing for export," but it carries FAO terms (non-commercial, credit required). For Senegal, Wikimedia Commons Category:Kayar has several pirogue-landing photos, but their per-file licenses were not checked. I found no Dakar port photo with verified details.

## Which photos show West African fishmeal factories (Mauritania, Senegal, Gambia)?

### Takeaway
Two strong, clearly West African options exist. (1) The FAO Digital Media Hub photo "GAMBIA, 2024. Sardinella fish processed at fishmeal factory" directly shows sardinella going into fishmeal, the exact story of this station. (2) Greenpeace's 2016 photos by Liu Yuyang from the Lem Seafood fishmeal/fish-processing factory in Nouadhibou were used in the Greenpeace report "A Waste of Fish" (2019). Both are copyrighted. I found no freely licensed (CC) photo of a West African fishmeal factory.

### Cited Findings
**Candidate 2: FAO, Gambia 2024, sardinella in a fishmeal factory (recommended for (b))**
- Title: "GAMBIA, 2024. Sardinella fish processed at fishmeal factory". Page: https://digital-media.fao.org/archive/GAMBIA--2024--Sardinella-fish-processed-at-fishmeal-factory-2A6XC5IRKOW9.html — [FAO Digital Media Hub](https://digital-media.fao.org/archive/GAMBIA--2024--Sardinella-fish-processed-at-fishmeal-factory-2A6XC5IRKOW9.html)

**Candidate 3: Greenpeace, Lem fishmeal/seafood factory, Nouadhibou, 2016**
- Per search snippets, the Greenpeace media archive has images of "local workers at the Lem Seafood factory in Nouadhibou, Mauritania" and "fish being processed at Lem Seafood factory". The credit is **"© Liu Yuyang / Greenpeace"**, and one image is dated **1 July 2016**. The caption context reads: "The Lem Fishmeal Factory in Nouadhibou is one of at least 20 fish meal factories in the town, half of which are Chinese owned. On average, the factory goes through 200 tonnes of fresh fish per day." — [Greenpeace Media: Chinese Fish Industry in West Africa](https://media.greenpeace.org/archive/Chinese-Fish-Industry-in-West-Africa-27MZIFJJL0XUV.html)
- Greenpeace commissioned Liu Yuyang in 2016 to spend a month documenting China's distant-water fishing industry in West Africa. — [Greenpeace Unearthed, 24 Nov 2016](https://unearthed.greenpeace.org/2016/11/24/fishing-inside-chinese-mega-industry-west-africa/)
- The photos appear in the Greenpeace report "A Waste of Fish: Food security under threat from the fishmeal and fish oil industry in West Africa" (June 2019). — [Greenpeace report PDF](https://www.greenpeace.org/static/planet4-international-stateless/2019/06/0bbe4b20-a-waste-of-fish-report-en-low-res.pdf)
- A related Greenpeace archive item is "Octopus in a Factory in Mauritania". It shows fish processing for export rather than fishmeal. — [Greenpeace Media](https://media.greenpeace.org/archive/Octopus-in-a-Factory-in-Mauritania-27MZIFLK5O_D.html)
- License: copyrighted (© photographer / Greenpeace). Greenpeace media archive images are generally released for editorial/press use with credit, but the **exact usage terms were not verified**.
- Uncertainty: the URL above is the search-indexed archive page titled "Chinese Fish Industry in West Africa". It is **not confirmed** to be the exact Lem factory image rather than another image from the same series.

**Other fishmeal-factory sources (no photo verified)**
- Greenpeace Africa report "Expanding Fishmeal Factories: A threat to food security & the environment" (2021, PDF), likely with photos of Gambian/Senegalese factories. — [Greenpeace PDF](https://www.greenpeace.org/static/planet4-africa-stateless/2021/02/c14f1ac5-expanding-fishmeal-factories.pdf)
- Dialogue Earth reports an aerial photo of the Golden Lead plant at Gunjur credited to the Changing Markets Foundation (credit wording from a search summary). — [Dialogue Earth](https://dialogue.earth/en/ocean/11980-fishmeal-factories-threaten-food-security-in-the-gambia/)
- Context for captions: Greenpeace's "A Waste of Fish" covers Mauritania, Senegal and Gambia, where about 50 factories had appeared. One Gambian factory, Golden Lead, produced output equal to 40% of Gambia's total fish catch for a given year. — [Dialogue Earth](https://dialogue.earth/en/ocean/11980-fishmeal-factories-threaten-food-security-in-the-gambia/)
- Other media covering Gunjur/Golden Lead, all copyrighted (photos not checked): [Mongabay 2021](https://news.mongabay.com/2021/04/a-fatal-stabbing-sends-a-gambian-fishing-village-into-turmoil-over-fishmeal/), [The New Humanitarian / Oceans Deeply 2018](https://deeply.thenewhumanitarian.org/oceans/articles/2018/07/12/gambians-fight-chinese-fishmeal-factory-as-fish-prices-soar-stocks-fall), [DeSmog 2023](https://www.desmog.com/2023/06/08/how-fishmeal-factories-put-food-out-of-reach-for-communities-in-the-gambia/), [VOA "Ocean Shock"](https://www.voanews.com/a/ocean-shock-fishmeal-factories-plunder-africa/4674766.html)

### Inferences
- The FAO Gambia photo matches the station's core message ("Sardinelle wird zu Fischmehl") better than any other find. The Greenpeace/Liu Yuyang photo is the best match for Nouadhibou.
- The only free-license (Commons) Gunjur result found was "Fishing boat, Gunjur Gambia banner.jpg" (CC BY 2.0 per snippet). It shows a fishing boat, **not** the factory, so it is only a generic substitute for Gambian landings. — [Commons file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fishing_boat,_Gunjur_Gambia_banner.jpg)

### Gaps
- No CC-licensed photo of any West African fishmeal factory was found. Searching Commons Category "Fish meal" directly was impossible because of the egress block.
- No Senegal fishmeal factory photo (Cayar/Kayar, Joal) was found.
- Photographer and factory identity for the FAO Gambia photo are unknown.
- The exact Greenpeace per-image page and its usage terms are unknown.
