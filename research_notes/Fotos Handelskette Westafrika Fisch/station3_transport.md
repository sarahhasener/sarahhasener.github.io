## Which photos show reefer ships / reefer containers at Las Palmas (Puerto de La Luz) or Vigo?

### Takeaway
I found **no indexed, freely licensed photo that explicitly shows a reefer ship or reefer containers in Las Palmas or Vigo**. The best freely licensed location-specific match is a CC BY-SA 4.0 photo of the OPCSA container terminal in Las Palmas (2020), which shows the container port where reefer boxes are handled but is not described as showing reefers specifically. For the "reefer ship" motif, the best free options are public-domain photos of reefer ships taken in Bremerhaven, which are **generic substitutes** (wrong location).

## Is there a usable photo of fish being shipped/flown from West Africa (e.g. reefer at Nouadhibou or Dakar)?

### Takeaway
I found no verified free photo of a reefer ship loading fish in Nouadhibou or Dakar, nor of fish air freight from West Africa. Freely licensed Nouadhibou photos exist on Commons, but they show the artisanal fishing port and the ship graveyard. NGO sources (WSRW, Greenpeace) document reefer ships in this trade, including stops in Las Palmas, but their photos are copyrighted and could not be inspected.

### Cited Findings
- Commons "Nouadhibou,PortArtisanal1.jpg": artisanal fishing port of Nouadhibou, Mauritania; uploaded by Bertramz, October 2010; licence **CC BY-SA 3.0** — [Wikimedia Commons (via search index)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nouadhibou,PortArtisanal1.jpg). Shows pirogues/artisanal port, **not** reefer shipping. Possibly better for an earlier station of the chain.
- Commons "Ships graveyard, Nouadhibou, Mauritania-2.jpg" and "-5.jpg": photographed 17 May 2004 by Sebastián Losada — [Wikimedia Commons -2](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ships_graveyard,_Nouadhibou,_Mauritania-2.jpg); [Wikimedia Commons -5](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ships_graveyard,_Nouadhibou,_Mauritania-5.jpg). Licence not stated in snippet. Background: the graveyard's largest wreck, United Malika, was a reefer ship that beached in 2003 — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/the-worlds-biggest-ship-graveyard/). Only indirectly related to the topic.
- Nouadhibou port exports include non-fillet frozen fish — [Marine Insight](https://www.marineinsight.com/know-more/major-ports-in-mauritania/)
- Western Sahara Resource Watch (WSRW) has documented reefer vessels carrying fish from waters off Western Sahara, and reports e.g. that the reefer "Green Austevoll" stopped in Las Palmas to bunker fuel; WSRW photographed "Admiral Starikov" bunkering at Las Palmas in 2008 — [WSRW: Green reefers aided Russia in plundering Saharawi fish](https://wsrw.org/en/news/green-reefers-aided-russia-in-plundering-saharawi-fish); [WSRW archive](https://wsrw.org/en/archive/4688) (via search snippets; photo licences unknown, assume © WSRW).
- Greenpeace UK photo essay on its West African fisheries investigation (illegal transshipment to reefers, e.g. "Saly Reefer" arrested by Guinea-Bissau inspectors) — [Medium / Greenpeace UK](https://medium.com/@GreenpeaceUK/in-pictures-greenpeaces-investigation-into-west-african-fisheries-ab0422657fa6); [Mongabay 2017](https://news.mongabay.com/2017/04/officials-greenpeace-nab-four-boats-for-illegally-fishing-near-guinea-bissau/amp/). Greenpeace photos are © Greenpeace; usable only with citation; individual photos not inspected.
- Getty Images has ~1,180 Nouadhibou stock photos — [Getty Images](https://www.gettyimages.com/photos/nouadhibou-mauritania). Commercial licence; not free.
- Air freight: only generic Commons files found, e.g. "Aircraft cargo (ULD) loader in operaton.jpg" — [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aircraft_cargo_(ULD)_loader_in_operaton.jpg). Nothing related to fish or West Africa; **generic substitute**, licence unverified.

### Inferences
- If a West Africa-side "transport" image is wanted, the WSRW or Greenpeace photos of reefers at sea are the most thematically accurate, but they are copyrighted (fine for a school presentation with source citation, not free).

### Gaps
- No verified photo of a reefer ship at the quay in Nouadhibou or Dakar.
- No verified photo of fresh fish/tuna air freight from West Africa (e.g. Dakar airport).
