## 1. Localization: physical location, hazards and administrative definitions

### Takeaway
Manila (CoM) lies at about 14°35′N, 120°58′E. It sits on the low deltaic plain of the Pasig River on the east shore of Manila Bay on Luzon. Its hazard profile is set by about 20 tropical cyclones a year in the Philippine area of responsibility and by the West Valley Fault (scenario of a magnitude 7.2 quake). Three official spatial units must be kept apart:
- CoM: 24.98 km²
- NCR / Metro Manila: 619.54 km² (PSA), created in 1975 by Presidential Decree 824
- Wider regions and functional areas: Calabarzon (Region IV-A), Central Luzon (Region III), and NEDA's "Mega Manila" or the "Greater Manila Area"

### Cited Findings
**Coordinates and site**
- Coordinates of CoM are 14°35′N 120°58′E. A more precise infobox value is 14°35′45″N 120°58′38″E (≈14.5958°N, 120.9772°E). One older mirror gives 121°00′E, which is probably outdated. [sek.] — [Wikiwand Manila](https://www.wikiwand.com/sco/articles/Manila); [Wikipedia mirror (archived)](https://webcf.waybackmachine.org/web/20220601025354/https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila)
- Britannica places Manila on the eastern side of Manila Bay, at the mouth of the Pasig River. The city "occupies the low, narrow deltaic plain of the Pasig River, which flows northwestward to Manila Bay." Britannica calls it the country's chief port and its economic, political and cultural centre. — [Britannica: Manila](https://www.britannica.com:443/place/Manila); [Britannica summary](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Manila)
- Laguna de Bay and the Marikina Valley were not described in any source the search returned. The Pasig River connects Laguna de Bay with Manila Bay. This is standard textbook knowledge, but **it was not verified in this session.**

**Natural hazards**
- PAGASA (official climate page): on average **20 tropical cyclones a year** enter the Philippine Area of Responsibility (PAR), and about 8–9 of them cross the Philippines. More tropical cyclones enter the PAR than any other region in the world. Season peak is July–October (about 70 % of cyclones). [official] — [PAGASA Tropical Cyclone Information](https://www.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/climate/tropical-cyclone-information); [Manila Bulletin 2026](https://mb.com.ph/2026/09/01/2-to-3-tropical-cyclones-may-develop-in-september-pagasa)
- The **West Valley Fault** (WVF) belongs to the Marikina Valley Fault System. It runs about 100 km from Bulacan through Metro Manila to Cavite and Laguna. PHIVOLCS scenario: a magnitude 7.2 earthquake ("The Big One") with Intensity VIII shaking in Metro Manila. The fault has moved about 4 times in the last 1,400 years, at intervals of about 400–600 years. The timing cannot be predicted. — [Philstar 2017](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2017/02/14/1671813/prepare-big-one/amp/); [Tribune 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/06/16/dost-chief-urges-preparedness-as-metro-manila-faces-quake-risks)
- Damage estimates differ between scenarios:
  - About 34,000 deaths and 170,000 collapsed houses (MMEIRS-based, 2015) — [Inquirer 2015](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/709030/the-big-one-could-kill-34000)
  - At least 168,000 collapsed buildings and more than 33,000 deaths (PHIVOLCS study, 2025) — [Tribune 2025](https://tribune.net.ph/2025/03/31/phivolcs-big-one-could-topple-168k-buildings-kill-over-33000)
  - About 5,000 deaths (GMA report) — [GMA](https://gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/941067/the-big-one-could-have-death-toll-of-50-000-phivolcs/story)
  - **These are scenario estimates, not forecasts.**

**Administrative structure**
- Metro Manila was created by **Presidential Decree No. 824, dated 7 November 1975** (Marcos, martial law). The decree restructured the local governments of the Greater Manila Area and set up the Metropolitan Manila Commission. Its territory was formed from 4 cities and 13 municipalities: 12 from Rizal province plus Valenzuela from Bulacan. — [jur.ph summary of PD 824](https://jur.ph/law/summary/creating-the-metropolitan-manila-and-the-metropolitan-manila-commission-and-for-other-purposes); [Wikiwand Metro Manila](https://wikiwand.com/en/Metro_Manila)
- Today NCR consists of 16 cities plus the municipality of Pateros, i.e. 17 LGUs. [sek.] The Manila Bulletin speaks of "Muntinlupa and the other 16 NCR LGUs". — [Manila Bulletin 2025](https://mb.com.ph/2025/07/20/muntinlupa-other-ncr-lgus-total-population-reaches-14-million)
- **Area of NCR (conflicting values):**
  - **619.54 km²** (PSA 2020 census release) **[PSA]** — [PSA RSSO-NCR 2020 highlights](https://rssoncr.psa.gov.ph/content/RSSO/highlights-national-capital-region-ncr-population-2020-census-population-and-housing-2020)
  - 619.57 km² (Wikipedia list of administrative divisions) [sek.]
  - 636.00 km² (other Wikipedia versions; also a 1985 journal article)
  - 613.5 km² (Outline of Metro Manila)
  - Recommendation: use the PSA value. — [Wikipedia: Administrative divisions of Metro Manila](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Metro_Manila); [PSSC 1985 PDF](https://pssc.org.ph/wp-content/pssc-archives/Philippine%20Journal%20of%20Public%20Administration/1985/Num%201/06_Metropolitan%20Government%20under%20Martial%20Law.pdf)
- Land area of CoM: **24.98 km²** **[PSA]** — [PSA RSSO-NCR: City of Manila 2020](https://rssoncr.psa.gov.ph/content/2020-census-population-and-housing-results-city-manila). One Wikipedia infobox gives a total area of 38.55 km² (land plus water/other), so land area and total area must not be confused. [sek.] — [Wikiwand](https://www.wikiwand.com/sco/articles/Manila)
- Capital status changed over time:
  - 1946: capital of the newly independent republic
  - 1948: Quezon City became the capital
  - 1976: Manila became the capital again
  - — [Britannica summary](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Manila)

**Wider region: competing definitions**
- **Calabarzon** (Region IV-A: Cavite, Laguna, Batangas, Rizal, Quezon) had **16.93 million** people in 2024 and is the most populous region, ahead of NCR. **Central Luzon** (Region III, including Bulacan) had **12.99 million** (2024 POPCEN). — PTV listing via search: [PTV News](https://ptvnews.ph/?p=243055); [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/2082959/palace-declares-2024-psa-census-results-as-official)
- **"Mega Manila"** as defined by NEDA = Metro Manila + Bulacan, Rizal, Cavite, Laguna. A JICA-linked study uses a **"Greater Capital Region"** = Metro Manila + Central Luzon + Calabarzon. — [Wikipedia: Mega Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mega_Manila); [UN DESA EGM, Laquian 2008](https://www.un.org/development/desa/pd/sites/www.un.org.development.desa.pd/files/unpd_egm_200801_the_planning_and_governance_of_asias_mega-urban_regions_laquian.pdf)
- **Greater Manila Area** (NCR + Bulacan, Cavite, Laguna, Rizal): about **26.7 million** people in 2020 (Wikipedia, summed from the 2020 census). [sek.] — [Wikipedia: Greater Manila Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Manila_Area)

### Inferences
- For the poster, a three-ring diagram works well: CoM (25 km²), then NCR (620 km²), then GMA / Mega Manila (four provinces). Each population figure should be tied to one of these rings.
- Its site in a river delta, on a bay, on a fault and in the typhoon belt makes Manila a textbook case of a "risk megacity".

- No official figure for the area of the GMA / Mega Manila was found.

## 2. Historical stages of urban development

### Takeaway
The stages are:
1. Pre-colonial Tagalog/Muslim settlement (Maynila, Tondo) on the Pasig delta
2. Spanish capital and walled city Intramuros from 24 June 1571
3. American colonial planning (Burnham Plan 1905, designed for 800,000 inhabitants)
4. Near-total destruction in the Battle of Manila (3 Feb – 3 Mar 1945)
5. Rapid post-war growth
6. Administrative creation of Metro Manila in 1975
7. Since then: polycentric private CBDs (Makati, Ortigas, BGC), suburbanization into Calabarzon and Bulacan, and land reclamation in Manila Bay

### Cited Findings
**Pre-colonial**
- The Laguna Copperplate Inscription points to a Tagalog polity called Tondo on the Pasig delta around 900 AD. [sek.] — [Wikipedia: History of Manila](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Colonial_Manila)
- Britannica describes the late 16th-century settlement as a walled Muslim settlement. Its ruler levied customs duties on all trade passing up the Pasig River. — [Britannica: History of Manila](https://www.britannica.com/place/Manila/History)
- The Tagalog principality of Maynila was an important regional trading centre. [sek.] — [New World Encyclopedia](https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Manila)
- The ruler Rajah Sulayman was **not confirmed** in any source the search returned.

**Spanish colonial period**
- Miguel López de Legazpi declared Manila the new capital of the Spanish colony on **24 June 1571**. Intramuros (the walled city) was founded after that. [sek.] — [Wikipedia: Intramuros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intramuros)

**American colonial period**
- Daniel Burnham and Pierce Anderson produced the plan in **1905**, when Manila had about 225,000 inhabitants. The plan was designed for a city of 800,000. It kept the walls of Intramuros but cut openings through the bastions for roads and turned the moat into sunken green space. [sek.] — [Wikipedia: Burnham Plan of Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnham_Plan_of_Manila); [CityMonitor](https://citymonitor.ai/environment/how-us-planners-experimented-iron-hand-power-over-colonial-manila-4610)
- Comparison with the census: 219,928 inhabitants of CoM in 1903 (see table in section 3). This roughly matches the "about 225,000" given in the Wikipedia article.

**Second World War**
- The **Battle of Manila** lasted from **3 February to 3 March 1945**. It was the only major urban battle of the Pacific War. — [Philstar 2020](https://www.philstar.com/opinion/2020/02/16/1993464/battle-manila-feb-3-march-3)
- About **100,000 civilians** were killed; some sources call this a conservative estimate. Intramuros was almost completely destroyed: six of seven churches were destroyed, and only San Agustín survived. — [Inquirer Global Nation](https://globalnation.inquirer.net/99054/february-1945-the-rape-of-manila); [Inquirer USA](https://usa.inquirer.net/117307/manilas-utter-destruction-during-world-war-ii)
- **Caution:** estimates of deaths and of the destroyed share of the city ("perhaps 70 %") vary a lot, and some sources are of low quality. How many deaths were caused by Japanese massacres and how many by American artillery is disputed.

**Post-war period and Metro Manila**
- The census years 1948–1960 brought very high growth (post-war reconstruction). Mandaluyong became part of the Greater Manila Area between 1960 and 1970. — [Mandaluyong city profile](https://mandaluyong.gov.ph/profile/population-dynamics)
- 1975: PD 824 created Metro Manila (see section 1). The Metropolitan Manila Commission was the metropolitan government under martial law. — [PSSC 1985](https://pssc.org.ph/wp-content/pssc-archives/Philippine%20Journal%20of%20Public%20Administration/1985/Num%201/06_Metropolitan%20Government%20under%20Martial%20Law.pdf)

**CBDs and private planning**
- Makati, Ortigas and Bonifacio (BGC) are the CBD clusters of NCR. Details on each were not verified in this session.
- Bonifacio Global City was built on the former **Fort Bonifacio** military base. A World Bank/GPSC case study calls it a luxury, gated project with limited public access for non-residents. — [GPSC case study Bonifacio (PDF)](https://www.thegpsc.org/sites/gpsc/files/7._bonifacio.pdf)
- Shatkin (2008, *Environment and Planning A* 40(2): 383–401) sees an "unprecedented privatization of urban and regional planning" as the defining feature of contemporary urban development in Metro Manila. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/envira/v40y2008i2p383-401.html); [Shatkin PDF](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1068/a38439)

**Land reclamation in Manila Bay**
- In August 2023 the DENR suspended the reclamation projects in Manila Bay for review. Media reports give different numbers: 22 projects nationwide, 16, or 13 in Manila Bay. — [GMA 2023](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/878699/only-3-ongoing-manila-bay-reclamation-projects-affected-by-suspension-pra/story); [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1817094/only-3-manila-bay-projects-ongoing-prior-to-halt-order-denr/amp)
- Projects and their size:
  - Pasay Harbour City: 265 ha
  - Pasay/SM Prime: 390 ha
  - Manila Waterfront City: 318 ha
  - Navotas Coastal Bay: 650 ha
  - Two projects (Pasay Harbour City and SM Prime) were allowed to resume in 2024.
  - — [GMA maps](https://gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/878555/maps-show-extent-of-manila-bay-reclamation-projects/story); [ABS-CBN 2024](https://news.abs-cbn.com/business/2024/2/28/2-manila-bay-reclamation-projects-expected-to-resume-soon-2057)

### Inferences
- The city has been reshaped from outside again and again: Spanish, US, Japanese, and then authoritarian (Marcos). Since the 1990s the shaping force has increasingly been private capital (Ayala, SM). For the poster this could be a chain of stages: colonial planning, then destruction, then rapid growth, then administrative metropolization, then privatized polycentrism.

### Gaps
- Exact founding dates of Makati CBD (Ayala, from the 1950s?), Ortigas and BGC (from the 1990s?) were **not verified**.
- The current status (Nov 2026) of each reclamation project was not checked.
- The full Britannica history page could not be opened.

## 3. Population time series and density

### Takeaway
- **CoM**: grew strongly up to 1980 (1.63 million), stagnated or fell slightly by 2000, and has grown again slightly since (1.90 million in 2024).
- **NCR**: grew from 3.97 million (1970) to **14,001,751** (2024 POPCEN, 1 July 2024).
- Growth is slowing: 0.91 % per year in 2020–2024, compared with 4.1 % per year in 1970–1980 [eigene Berechnung].
- The **PSA 2024 POPCEN results have been published.** They were declared official by Proclamation No. 973 in July 2025.
- **UN WUP 2025:** the Manila urban agglomeration had about 24.7 million inhabitants in 2025 (rank 8 in the world). This uses a new, much wider definition.
- CoM is extremely dense: **73,920 inhabitants per km² (2020, PSA)**.

  - NCR ≈ 22,600 inhabitants per km² (14,001,751 ÷ 619.54)
  - The PSA had not published an official density for 2024 in the results found.
- A Wikipedia-derived table gives about 43,000 per km² for 42.88 km². This uses **a different area definition** and must not be mixed with the PSA figure. [sek.]

### Inferences
- The UN-UA figure for 2025 (24.7 million) is about 1.8 times the NCR figure (14.0 million). The difference shows how far the built-up agglomeration has grown beyond the administrative NCR boundary. This is an important teaching point for "Metropolisierung".
- CoM has been close to saturated since 1980 (stagnation or slight decline, then slight regrowth). Growth has moved to the outer NCR cities (Quezon City, Mandaluyong, Taguig) and to the neighbouring provinces. Since 2015, Central Luzon and Calabarzon have grown faster than NCR. This points to suburbanization / peri-urbanization.
- CoM's density (73,920 per km²) is among the highest for cities worldwide. A direct worldwide ranking was **not verified** in this session; do not write "densest city in the world" without a source.

### Gaps
- The 1903–1960 figures for CoM and the "Metro" figures come only from Wikipedia. The original census volumes (e.g. [US Census 1903](https://www.census.gov/library/publications/1905/dec/1903-census-philippine-islands.html)) were not checked.
- There is no NCR figure for 1939, and it is unclear which boundary the historical "Metro" values use.
- The 2024 figure for CoM (1,902,590) is only confirmed through a Manila Bulletin report, not the PSA table.
- The UN WUP 2025 figures come from secondary reproductions; the UN data portal could not be reached. The 1950 value and earlier WUP 2018 values (which used a narrower definition) were not found.

## 4. Natural population change vs. migration

### Takeaway
NCR has the **lowest fertility of any region** in the country (TFR 1.2 in 2022, below replacement level). Today's growth (0.9 % per year) therefore comes mainly from population momentum and in-migration. NCR is still the most important single destination region for internal migrants (2018 National Migration Survey). However, some data show **net out-migration** from NCR to the neighbouring regions (Calabarzon, Central Luzon), which fits suburbanization.

### Cited Findings
**Fertility**
- **TFR in NCR (2022 NDHS) = 1.2 children per woman**, the lowest value of any region. National TFR = 1.9; BARMM = 3.1. — [DHS Program, 2022 Philippines NDHS Summary Report (SR276)](https://www.dhsprogram.com/pubs/pdf/SR276/SR276.pdf)
- National TFR fell from 2.7 (2017) to 1.9 (2022). **[PSA]** — [PSA: TFR declined](https://psa.gov.ph/content/total-fertility-rate-declined-27-2017-19-2022)

**Migration (2018 National Migration Survey, PSA/UPPI)**
- Types of lifetime migration:
  - rural → rural: 46.4 %
  - rural → urban: 35.3 %
  - urban → urban: 13.4 %
  - urban → rural: 5.0 %
- NCR was the most important destination region. NCR received the most interregional in-migrants (388,000), followed by Calabarzon (293,000). — [PSA RSSO-CAR: 2018 NMS](https://rssocar.psa.gov.ph/content/2018-national-migration-survey-migration-experiences-filipinos)

**Net flows: contradictory**
- One table in a PIDS paper shows a **net loss** for NCR (2018): in-migrants 121,784 and out-migrants 292,484, giving a net of −170,700. — [PIDS DP 2023-20](https://pidswebs.pids.gov.ph/CDN/document/pidsdps2320.pdf)
- Another source names NCR (+1.77 million) and Calabarzon as the regions with the largest net gains. This is probably a different reference basis (lifetime vs. recent migration).
- BSP paper: in 2013–2018, internal migration shifted towards urban → rural for the first time, and NCR is listed among the net-loss regions. — [BSP DP 2023-01](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Sites/researchsite/Publications/BSP-Discussion-Papers/DP202301.pdf)

**Push and pull factors (qualitative)**
- Pull:
  - Concentration of national wealth and jobs in Manila: "makes it a magnet for migrants"
  - Education, as for migrants from Cebu — [RSU 2016 (Cebuano migrants)](https://rsucon.rsu.ac.th/files/proceedings/inter2016/G4-13.pdf)
  - Manila as a springboard for jobs abroad (a Plan International study)
- Lack of affordable housing within the city leads to informal settlement. — [IAFOR 2023 (Informal settlements Manila)](https://papers.iafor.org/wp-content/uploads/papers/apisa2023/APISA2023_71134.pdf)
- After the pandemic, people have reportedly moved out of NCR because of lower living costs and jobs elsewhere. **This is only an industry/broker claim, not survey data.** — [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/more-people-are-leaving-metro-manila-heres-why/)

### Inferences
- Phase model (interpretation): up to about 1980, very high growth driven by rural–urban migration plus high natural increase. Since the 2000s, falling fertility (TFR 1.2) and net out-migration to the suburbs. NCR's growth is flattening, and growth of the metropolitan region is moving to Calabarzon and Bulacan.
- Circular migration (seasonal / commuting) is plausible, given the large commuter flows from the neighbouring provinces into NCR.

### Gaps
- **No time series** was found for the share of migrants in NCR's growth (e.g. 1960s–1980s). No PSA figures for crude birth or death rates in NCR were retrieved.
- No source with figures on circular migration or commuter flows between Calabarzon / Bulacan and NCR was found.
- The conflicting net migration figures for NCR could not be resolved without the original tables.

## 5. Characteristic processes and spatial structures

### Takeaway
- **Primacy and concentration:** In 2024 NCR had 12.4 % of the population but **31.2 % of the national GDP**. Services make up 82.9 % of its economy, so it is strongly tertiarized.
- **Informal settlements:** about **596,000 informal settler families** in NCR (DHSUD data, reported by PIDS around 2024).
- **Fragmentation:** gated communities and private master-planned CBDs (Shatkin: "privatization of planning").
- **Peri-urbanization:** the built-up area grows mainly in the neighbouring provinces (NASA: 2000–2010 urban area grew from about 1,800 to 2,300 km²).
- **Model:** McGee's desakota / extended metropolitan region concept describes the rural–urban mixed zone in the Calabarzon / Bulacan periphery well.

### Cited Findings
**Primacy and economic concentration**
- NCR's GRDP in 2024 was **₱6.94 trillion** (at constant 2018 prices), the largest share of the national economy at **31.2 %**. The economy grew 5.6 % (2023: 4.9 %). Sector shares: services **82.9 %**, industry **17.1 %**, agriculture ≈0.01 %. — [PSA RSSO-NCR: NCR's economy increases 5.6 percent 2024](https://rssoncr.psa.gov.ph/content/ncrs-economy-increases-56-percent-2024); [Philstar 2025](https://www.philstar.com/business/2025/04/23/2437599/ncr-economy-grows-56-percent-2024)
  - **Caution:** a BusinessWorld report on the 2023 data also gives 31.2 %, so check against the original PSA release.
- Quezon City alone: ₱1.33 trillion, about 6 % of national GDP (2024). — [BusinessWorld/Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/tr/stories/newspaper/Business-World-Philippines/QUEZON-CITY-NOW-ACCOUNTS-FOR-6-OF-PHILIPPINE-GDP)
- World Bank (Philippines Urbanization Review, 2017):
  - Philippine cities produce more than 70 % of national output, more than half of it from Metro Manila alone.
  - About 45 % of Filipinos live in urban areas.
  - The urban population is projected at 102 million (about 65 %) by 2050.
  - — [World Bank press release 2017](https://worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2017/05/29/philippines-building-competitive-sustainable-and-inclusive-cities); [Inquirer 2017](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/900745/world-bank-filipinos-living-in-urban-areas-to-double-to-102m-by-2050); [full report (txt)](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/963061495807736752/text/114088-REVISED-PUBLIC-Philippines-Urbanization-Review-Full-Report.txt)
  - **Note:** "more than half of the urban output" (World Bank, about 2015) and "31.2 % of national GDP" (PSA 2024) have different reference bases (urban output vs. national GDP). They do not contradict each other.

**Informal settlements**
- DHSUD data, reported by PIDS around late 2024: **596,308 informal settler families (ISFs)** in NCR, occupying 3,447.93 ha. — [PIDS (in the news)](https://pids.gov.ph/details/news/in-the-news/phl-has-largest-slum-area-in-the-world)
  - The cut-off date of the data is unclear. A DHSUD eFOI reply refers to Local Shelter Plan data "as of December 2019". — [FOI DHSUD](https://foi.gov.ph/agencies/dhsud/number-of-isfs-in-the-philippines)
- Older figures, with different methods:
  - NHA 2011: 584,425 families in Metro Manila (Quezon City alone: 232,181)
  - NEDA about 2010/11: 556,526
  - Danger zones (NHA via HUDCC 2014): 104,219 ISFs in Metro Manila, about 60,130 of them along waterways
  - — [HUDCC 2014](https://hudcc.gov.ph/node/178); [ADB 2012 presentation (PDF)](https://events.development.asia/system/files/materials/2012/04/201204-socialized-housing-program.pdf)
- UN-Habitat (via BusinessWorld): about 3.7 million ISFs nationwide, "half of which" live in slums and high-risk areas in Metro Manila. **This figure seems high and its reference is unclear; check before using.** — [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/?p=666792)
- Claim "PHL has largest slum area in the world": VERA Files did a fact check on it. **Do not take it over unchecked.** — [VERA Files](https://verafiles.org/articles/vera-files-fact-check-does-metro-manila-have-worlds-largest)
- Policy: under the 4PH programme, about 170,000 housing units are planned in 55 projects in Metro Manila (2024). Since Dec 2025 there has been a "high-density" component that gives priority to danger zones. — [Manila Bulletin 2024](https://mb.com.ph/2024/1/10/gov-t-to-build-170-000-housing-units-in-ncr-metro-mayors-tasked-to-find-temporary-shelters); [Manila Bulletin 2025](https://mb.com.ph/2025/12/12/dhsud-expands-4ph-program-with-high-density-housing)
- More than 70,000 ISFs were resettled for the Manila Bay rehabilitation (Inquirer, about 2019). — [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1185462/more-than-70k-informal-settlers-relocated-for-manila-bay-rehab)

**Fragmentation, gated communities and private planning**
- Shatkin (2008) describes the transfer of power over visioning urban futures from public to private actors. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/envira/v40y2008i2p383-401.html)
- Mouton & Shatkin (2020, *Env. & Planning A* 52(2)) describe a shift from "privatization of planning" to "planning of privatization". — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:envira:v:52:y:2020:i:2:p:403-422)
- BGC is a luxury, gated project with limited public access. — [GPSC Bonifacio (PDF)](https://www.thegpsc.org/sites/gpsc/files/7._bonifacio.pdf)
- A study on Makati: "The Ideology of the Dual City: The Modernist Ethic in the Corporate Development of Makati City". — [Deep Blue UMich](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/handle/2027.42/95410?show=full)
- A Kasarinlan article argues that development moved from "spaces of exception" (gated communities) to entirely exclusive developments. — [Kasarinlan (UP)](https://journals.upd.edu.ph/index.php/kasarinlan/article/download/9587/8459)

**Peri-urbanization and the mega-urban region**
- NASA Earth Observatory ("The Growth of Manila", Landsat 1988 vs. 2014): urban area grew from about 1,800 to 2,300 km² between 2000 and 2010, **mostly in the neighbouring provinces, not in Manila itself**. — [NASA Earth Observatory: The Growth of Manila](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/the-growth-of-manila-86780)
- Built-up area is spreading over southern Bulacan, western Laguna, northern Cavite and western Rizal. Large subdivisions, malls and mixed-use complexes are replacing farmland. [sek.] — [Wikipedia: Greater Manila Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Manila_Area)
- GHSL-based built-up area for "Manila" (Statista, reference unit unclear): about 880.93 km² (2000) and about 932.33 km² (2015). — [Statista](https://www.Statista.com/statistics/911713/philippines-built-up-area-manila/)
  - **This differs a lot from NASA's 1,800–2,300 km²** because of different definitions and methods.
- ISPRS 2023 (GHSL, Philippines 1975–2020): urban regions more than tripled in size. No Manila-specific values were in the extract. — [ISPRS Annals 2023](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-1-W1-2023/413/2023/)

**Urban structure model**
- McGee (1991) coined **"desakota"** (Bahasa Indonesia *desa* = village, *kota* = town) for peri-urban zones with dense rural–urban mixed use. It is part of his concept of the "extended metropolitan region" (core, peri-urban desakota zone, outer fringe). It is explained by:
  - location in densely populated wet-rice regions
  - transactive environments in the urban periphery
  - the readiness of rural labour for industrial work
  - — [UN DESA EGM 2008, Laquian](https://www.un.org/development/desa/pd/sites/www.un.org.development.desa.pd/files/unpd_egm_200801_the_planning_and_governance_of_asias_mega-urban_regions_laquian.pdf); [Mapping Manila's Mega-Urban Region (ResearchGate)](https://researchgate.net/publication/271937749_Mapping_Manila's_Mega-Urban_Region)
- Researchers call Manila a "mega-urban region" (MUR). Desakota works as a "spatial hinge" where the urban–rural dichotomy dissolves. — [Mapping Manila's Mega-Urban Region](https://researchgate.net/publication/271937749_Mapping_Manila's_Mega-Urban_Region)

### Inferences
- **Primacy (interpretation):** NCR has 12.4 % of the population but 31.2 % of GDP, so value added per head is about 2.5 times the national average **[eigene Berechnung: 31.2 ÷ 12.4]**. This shows strong economic primacy. A classical primacy index (largest city ÷ second-largest city) is hard to compute because the units are not comparable (NCR is a conurbation, Quezon City lies inside NCR, Cebu and Davao are defined differently). **Not verified with a source.**
- **Structural model for the poster:**
  - Core: CoM and Intramuros (colonial old town, high density)
  - Ring: polycentric CBD network (Makati, Ortigas, BGC), largely private
  - Mixed in: informal settlements along waterways, rail lines and the coast (danger zones)
  - Outside: suburban subdivisions and industrial estates in Calabarzon and Bulacan, forming a desakota-like mixed zone
  - This combines McGee's model with fragmentation (Shatkin). It is an **interpretation**, not a model taken from one single source.

### Gaps
- The world ranking for CoM's density, and a sourced primacy index, were not found.
- There is no reliable current NHA / DHSUD time series of ISFs (2019 vs. 2024 is unclear).
- There are no quantitative data on the number of gated communities or on tertiarization over time (e.g. employment by sector in NCR, 1990 vs. today).
- The JICA "Roadmap for Transport Infrastructure Development for Metro Manila and Its Surrounding Areas" (Dream Plan, 2014) was **not retrieved** in this session.
