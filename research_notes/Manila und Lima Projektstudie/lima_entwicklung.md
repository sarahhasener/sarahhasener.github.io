## 1. Localization (Lage, Naturraum, Risiken, Verwaltung)

### Takeaway
Lima lies on the arid central Pacific coast of Peru. It occupies the valleys and alluvial fans of the Rímac, Chillón and Lurín rivers, between the ocean and the Andes foothills. The cold Humboldt Current produces a fog-dominated (garúa) hyper-arid climate. The city sits on the Central Peru subduction zone, and great earthquakes such as 1746, 1940 and 1974 have struck it repeatedly. The metropolitan area has 50 districts: 43 in the Provincia de Lima and 7 in Callao.

### Cited Findings
- **Rivers and site:** The territory formed by the valleys of the Rímac, Chillón and Lurín was settled by pre-Inca groups before 1535. The Spanish city was founded in the Rímac valley. — [Expreso (huacas de Lima)](https://www.expreso.com.pe/actualidad/un-recorrido-por-las-huacas-que-cuentan-la-historia-de-lima-antes-de-la-llegada-de-francisco-pizarro-el-valle-del-rimac-ya-estaba-poblado-huaca-el-paraiso-complejo-mateo-salado-noticia/1255924/); [EcuRed Lima](https://www.ecured.cu/Lima)
- **NASA ISS photo caption (March 2005, Expedition 10):** Lima is "located on the broad alluvial fan of the Rimac River". It is called "the only mega city" on South America's west coast, with about 7.7 million residents (2002, unit not specified). Mid-20th-century growth is attributed to "a significant relocation of people from rural mountain communities". — [Google Arts & Culture / NASA ISS010-E-20111](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/earth-observations-taken-by-the-expedition-10-crew/7wHxCuceADbH6g?hl=en); [NASA image viewer mirror](https://nasa-img-viewer.pages.dev/image/iss010e20111)
- **Humboldt Current and garúa:** The cold Humboldt Current creates both the coastal desert and the garúa fog along the Peruvian and Chilean coast between about 5° and 30° S. — [Wikipedia: Garúa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gar%C3%BAa)
- **Annual rainfall:** Wikipedia "Climate of Peru" lists Lima's annual precipitation as 16 mm. Other web sources give about 5–15 mm. — [Wikipedia: Climate of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Peru)
  - Climate-data.org gives about 203 mm, which is implausible for Lima and should not be used. — [climate-data.org Lima](https://en.climate-data.org/south-america/peru/lima-1038/)
  - No SENAMHI station value was retrieved.
- **"Desert city" claim:** The common claim "second-largest desert city after Cairo" was **not verified**. The only source found is a low-authority blog. It says something different, namely "second driest capital after Cairo". — [The Andean Post (low authority)](https://subsequently.post.edu.pe/)
- **Earthquake of 28 Oct 1746 (Lima–Callao):**
  - Magnitude estimates are about Mw 8.6–8.8 (Wikipedia) and up to Mw 9.0 (a modelling paper).
  - The tsunami destroyed Callao, with a run-up of up to about 24 m listed.
  - Death tolls conflict: about 1,141 dead in Lima out of about 60,000 inhabitants, and about 5,000–6,000 in Callao. — [Wikipedia: 1746 Lima–Callao earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1746_Lima%E2%80%93Callao_earthquake); [Jimenez et al., seismic source of 1746](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/b720/e30fd51cdbee51bad98b948c97f92e3b9e4a.pdf); [Wikipedia: List of earthquakes in Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_earthquakes_in_Peru)
- **Earthquake of 24 May 1940:**
  - Mw 8.2, with a tsunami of up to about 2 m.
  - Its rupture zone lies between those of 1966 and 1974.
  - Death tolls conflict between sources: 562 in the earthquake list, versus a figure of 58 for Callao on another page. — [Wikipedia: 1940 Lima earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1940_Lima_earthquake); [List of earthquakes in Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_earthquakes_in_Peru)
- **Earthquake of 3 Oct 1974:** Mw 8.1, 78 deaths, 2,400 injured. This rests on a single list source. — [Wikipedia: List of earthquakes in Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_earthquakes_in_Peru)
- **Tectonic setting:** The 1746 event is modelled as a megathrust in the "Central Peru subduction zone". — [Jimenez et al.](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/b720/e30fd51cdbee51bad98b948c97f92e3b9e4a.pdf)
- **Recent event:** Search results also show a Wikipedia page "2025 Callao earthquake". Its content was not retrieved. — [Wikipedia: 2025 Callao earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Callao_earthquake)
- **Administrative structure:** The metropolitan area has 50 districts: 43 in the Provincia de Lima and 7 in the Provincia Constitucional del Callao. — [Hispanopedia mirror of Spanish Wikipedia "Área Metropolitana de Lima"](https://es.hispanopedia.com/wiki/%C3%81rea_Metropolitana_de_Lima)
- **Area:**
  - Lima + Callao = 2,819 km². — [CAF Urban Mobility Observatory – Lima](https://caf.com/en/topics/u/urban-mobility-observatory/cities-information/lima); [Hispanopedia](https://es.hispanopedia.com/wiki/Lima_Metropolitana)
  - Provincia de Lima alone = 2,672.28 km². — [Hispanopedia "Lima"](https://es.hispanopedia.com/wiki/Lima)
  - Callao's area by subtraction would be about 147 km². This is my own arithmetic, not a sourced figure.
- **Largest district:** Carabayllo, with 346.9 km², followed by Ancón and Cieneguilla. — [El Comercio](https://elcomercio.pe/respuestas/trends/cual-es-el-distrito-mas-grande-y-pequeno-de-lima-la-respuesta-te-sorprendera-tdpe-noticia-2)
- **Density (INEI, 2023 projection, "Lima Metropolitana"):** 3,879.1 inhabitants/km². Surquillo is the densest district. Punta Negra is the least dense, at 69.6 inhabitants/km². — [INEI Nota de prensa 006-2023](https://m.inei.gob.pe/media/MenuRecursivo/noticias/nota-de-prensa-no-006-2023-inei.pdf)

### Inferences
- The site combines a coastal desert, three river oases and the Andean piedmont. This explains two things:
  - Expansion follows the river valleys (Chillón → Lima Norte; Rímac → Lima Este; Lurín → Lima Sur) and climbs onto hillsides.
  - Water scarcity is a structural constraint on the city.
- Very low rainfall partly explains why self-built, flat-roofed, unfinished houses were viable on desert land.

### Gaps
- Exact coordinates were not retrieved. The commonly cited value is about 12°03′ S, 77°02′ W; verify it on Britannica or Wikipedia before use.
- No SENAMHI rainfall figure was found.
- The "largest desert city" ranking is unverified. Do not use it without a reliable source.
- No official INEI area figure was found for Lima Metropolitana.
- Death tolls for 1940 and 1974 conflict between sources.

## 2. Historical stages (pre-Hispanic → 2020s)

### Takeaway
Lima moved through several stages:
1. A pre-Hispanic oasis landscape with huacas.
2. A Spanish foundation (1535), then a walled viceregal capital.
3. Leguía's modernisation in the 1920s, which created avenues toward the coast.
4. An explosive "desborde popular" from the 1940s–50s, through barriadas and pueblos jóvenes (VES 1971), reinforced by war displacement in 1980–2000.
5. In the 1990s: neoliberal formalisation, gated enclaves and polycentric "conos".
6. Since about 2017: Venezuelan immigration.

### Cited Findings
- **Pre-Hispanic period:**
  - Huaca Pucllana is a large ceremonial centre built in the 5th–7th centuries AD by the "Lima" population, which lived on the central coast between 200 and 700 AD (Museo de Sitio leaflet). — [Ministerio de Cultura (museamos) tríptico](https://museamos.cultura.pe/sites/default/files/publicaciones/pdf/tr%C3%ADptico%20MSP.pdf)
  - For decades the site was used as a dump. Research and restoration began in the 1980s. — [Expreso](https://www.expreso.com.pe/actualidad/un-recorrido-por-las-huacas-que-cuentan-la-historia-de-lima-antes-de-la-llegada-de-francisco-pizarro-el-valle-del-rimac-ya-estaba-poblado-huaca-el-paraiso-complejo-mateo-salado-noticia/1255924/)
  - Further reading: [PUCP "Lima Milenaria" brochure](https://ira.pucp.edu.pe/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/BROCHURE-LIMA-MILENARIA.pdf)
- **Spanish foundation:** Francisco Pizarro founded the city on 18 Jan 1535 in the Rímac valley as "Ciudad de los Reyes". — [EcuRed Lima](https://www.ecured.cu/Lima); [Expreso, 489 años](https://www.expreso.com.pe/actualidad/489-anos-de-fundacion-la-historia-de-lima-la-tres-veces-coronada-ciudad-de-los-reyes-francisco-pizarro-noticia/1060957/)
- **City walls:** Viceroy Melchor de Navarra y Rocafull built the city walls in 1684–1687 against pirates and corsairs in the Pacific. — [EcuRed Lima](https://www.ecured.cu/Lima)
- **Leguía's "Oncenio" (1919–1930):**
  - The urban area roughly doubled.
  - The triangle Lima–Callao–Chorrillos began to be occupied.
  - The avenues Leguía, El Progreso and Costanera consolidated growth, and more than 30 urbanizaciones were planned (incl. San Isidro, Chorrillos, Miramar). — [Quispe, Arquitextos (URP) 2020](https://revistas.urp.edu.pe/index.php/Arquitextos/article/download/3893/4771/12198)
  - The three axes were Av. Leguía (now Arequipa), Av. Brasil and Av. Progreso (now Colonial). — [Revista Ulima](https://revistas.ulima.edu.pe/index.php/Limaq/article/download/7187/7675)
  - The law was changed to allow expropriation of up to 100 m on each side of interurban avenues. — [La República 2024](https://larepublica.pe/sociedad/2024/04/08/av-arequipa-una-de-las-vias-mas-concurridas-del-peru-como-se-llamaba-y-por-que-se-le-cambio-de-nombre-99392)
- **Barriadas (Matos Mar):**
  - 1956: barriadas housed 119,886 people, 9.5% of Lima Metropolitana's population.
  - 1959: 236,716 people, 14.3%.
  - 1961 (Ley de Barriadas No. 13517): 17% of Lima's population lived in barriadas.
  - By 2010: more than 3,000 barriadas in Lima Metropolitana, with more than 36.5% of its population.
  - (Figures quoted from Matos Mar's work in secondary sources.) — [Matos Mar, *Desborde popular* PDF (uv.mx)](https://www.uv.mx/jose-marti/files/2018/08/Jose-Matos-Mar-Desborde-popular.pdf); [Matos Mar, "Una barriada nueva cada día", El Comercio](https://elcomercio.pe/opinion/columnistas/barriada-nueva-dia-jose-matos-mar-356225-noticia/); [limamalalima blog on Ley 13517](https://limamalalima.wordpress.com/2021/02/18/ley-no-13517-60-anos-de-la-ley-que-partio-en-dos-la-historia-de-las-ciudades-peruanas/)
  - Discrepancy: Matos Mar's El Comercio column gives the 1956 figure as "56 barriadas, about 10%" rather than 9.5%.
- **Villa El Salvador:**
  - Families came from the Pamplona land invasion (mass occupation on about 28 Apr 1971).
  - On 11 May 1971 they were relocated to the sands of Tablada de Lurín, which is the founding date of VES.
  - VES became a legal district by Decreto Ley 23605 on 1 Jun 1983. — [La República 2022](https://larepublica.pe/sociedad/2022/12/26/como-se-veia-villa-el-salvador-en-1971-y-por-que-el-distrito-se-llama-asi-lima-metropolitana-historia-de-lima-pamplona); [Wikipedia ES: Distrito de Villa El Salvador](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distrito_de_Villa_El_Salvador); [CyTET: Organización vecinal y diseño urbano: VES](https://recyt.fecyt.es/index.php/CyTET/article/download/83929/61972/276458)
- **Internal armed conflict 1980–2000:**
  - The CVR estimates about 500,000 internally displaced people.
  - The Lugar de la Memoria (LUM) gives more than 600,000; academic estimates range from 430,000 to 600,000.
  - Ayacucho was the most affected department. Lima was a major destination. — [Congreso del Perú, presentación desplazamiento](https://www3.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/sites/webs/cip/materiales/desplazamiento/Presentacion.pdf); [LUM catálogo 2016](https://lum.cultura.pe/cdi/sites/default/files/libro/pdf/2016%20Cat%C3%A1logo%20de%20la%20muestra%20permanente%20del%20LUM.pdf); [Ramírez Zapata, ALACIP](https://alacip.org/cong15/pra-zapata8c.pdf); [CVR Informe Final, Tomo IV, "La región Lima Metropolitana"](https://www.cverdad.org.pe/ifinal/pdf/TOMO%20IV/SECCION%20TERCERA-Los%20Escenarios%20de%20la%20Violencia/Historias%20Regionales/1.5%20REGION%20LIMA%20METROPOLITANA.pdf)
- **1990s neoliberal context:** Plöger argues that Lima's residential enclaves must be understood in the context of the 1980s recession and 1990s structural adjustment. His work comes from a DFG project at the Geographisches Institut, Universität Kiel, on the transformation of Latin American cities. — [Plöger 2007, Cybergeo](https://journals.openedition.org/cybergeo/6785); [LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/3610)
- **COFOPRI:**
  - Created in 1996 for a national urban titling programme.
  - Simplified registration in Lima's informal settlements cut the time to a title from about 7 years to 45 days (Lincoln Institute).
  - Since its creation it has issued "about two million" titles. — [Lincoln Institute, "Poseer y conservar"](https://www.lincolninst.edu/es/publications/articles/poseer-conservar/)
  - A FLACSO thesis states the 1996–2003 targets as 960,000 registered properties and 805,500 individual titles; the sum issued is garbled in the original. — [FLACSO thesis 2010](https://repositorio.flacsoandes.edu.ec/bitstream/10469/2842/1/TFLACSO-2010CAEO.pdf)
  - A literature review citing Calderón (2001–2007) finds no clear link between titling and access to credit in Peru. — [Estudios Demográficos y Urbanos (Colmex)](https://estudiosdemograficosyurbanos.colmex.mx/index.php/edu/article/download/1362/1355)
  - Independent evaluation for the MEF: [Informe final COFOPRI 2010](https://www.mef.gob.pe/contenidos/presu_publ/ppr/eval_indep/2010_informe_final_COFOPRI.pdf)
- **Metropolitan planning:**
  - The *Plan de Desarrollo Metropolitano de Lima 2021–2040* (PLANMET 2040) was approved by Ordenanza N° 2499-2022 (published Sept 2022).
  - It includes 471 projects worth about S/ 327,865 million, organised in five strategic axes.
  - It aims to create new zones concentrating jobs and services, i.e. a polycentric model. — [El Peruano – Ordenanza 2499-2022](https://busquedas.elperuano.pe/normaslegales/aprueban-el-plan-de-desarrollo-metropolitano-de-lima-2021-20-ordenanza-no-2499-2022-2105825-1/); [Andina](https://andina.pe/ingles/noticia-plan-metropolitano-lima-al-2040-suma-inversiones-s-327865-millones-908331.aspx); [Gestión](https://gestion.pe/peru/mml-plan-de-desarrollo-urbano-2021-2040-propone-generar-nuevas-zonas-que-concentren-empleos-y-servicios-nndc-noticia/); [Executive summary PDF (CIDEU)](https://www.cideu.org/wp-content/uploads/R_Plan-Desarrollo-Metropolitano-Lima-2021-40-1.pdf)
  - The earlier PLAM 2035 (Lima + Callao, 2014–15) was never formally adopted; this is commonly reported but not verified in this session. — [PLAM2035 (UN-Habitat host)](https://unhabitat.org/sites/default/files/download-manager-files/PLAM2035_150614.pdf)

### Inferences
- The stages fit the Bähr/Mertins and Borsdorf sequence:
  - colonial compact city;
  - sectoral elite expansion along avenues toward the coast (Leguía → San Isidro/Miraflores);
  - peripheral marginal belt (barriadas → conos);
  - fragmented city (enclaves, malls, walls).

### Gaps
- Independence (28 Jul 1821) was not checked in this session; it is a standard date.
- No source was retrieved on wall demolition (1868–70).
- No source was retrieved on the founding dates of Comas or San Martín de Porres barriadas.
- No figure for Lima-specific war displacement was found. The CVR Tomo VI would be the source.

## 3. Population time series

### Takeaway
Lima Metropolitana (Lima + Callao, INEI) grew as follows:
- about 0.65 million (1940)
- about 1.85 million (1961)
- about 3.3 million (1972)
- about 4.6 million (1981)
- about 6.35 million (1993)
- about 9.57 million (2017)
- about 10.1 million (2025 census, first results)

This is roughly a fifteen-fold increase in 85 years. Lima holds about 29–30% of Peru's population. **Watch out:** INEI 2025 releases give two different Lima figures (10.13 million and 9.6 million). The difference is probably due to the reference unit, but this is unconfirmed.

### Other cited findings
- **2017 census, Provincia de Lima:**
  - 8,567,786 urban residents, 99.9% of the province.
  - The province held about 29.2% of Peru's censused population, or 29.3% incl. omission.
  - Largest districts: San Juan de Lurigancho 1,038,495; San Martín de Porres 654,083; Ate 599,196; Comas 520,450. — [INEI Lib1583](https://www.inei.gob.pe/media/MenuRecursivo/publicaciones_digitales/Est/Lib1583/15ATOMO_01.pdf)
- **2017 census, Callao:** intercensal growth of 1.3%/yr (2007–2017). — [INEI Nota de prensa 108-2018](https://www.inei.gob.pe/media/MenuRecursivo/noticias/nota-de-prensa-no-108-2018-inei.pdf)
- **2025 census, districts:**
  - San Juan de Lurigancho remains Peru's most populous district with 1,125,000.
  - Then San Martín de Porres 724,000; Ate 669,000; Comas 553,000; Callao district 456,000.
  - Carabayllo (Lima Norte) grew 2.9%/yr (2017–2025), one of the three fastest-growing districts in Peru.
  - 65.5% of foreigners counted reside in Lima Metropolitana. — [Infobae, 10 Jul 2026](https://www.infobae.com/peru/2026/07/10/censo-nacional-2025-lima-metropolitana-concentra-el-mayor-crecimiento-poblacional/)
- **2025 census, national:** Peru totals 34,157,732. That is +2.92 million since 2017, at 1.11%/yr. — [El Peruano](https://elperuano.pe/noticia/296847-peru-numero-de-habitantes-asciende-a-34-millones-157-mil-732-informa-el-inei)

### Inferences

## 4. Natural growth vs. migration; Venezuelan immigration

### Takeaway
Rural–urban migration from the Andes drove Lima's growth from the 1940s to the 1990s, intensified by war displacement in 1980–2000. Today about one third of residents were born elsewhere in Peru. Fertility is now very low (TFR 1.22 in Lima Metropolitana, 2024), so growth increasingly depends on in-migration, including about 1 million Venezuelans.

### Cited Findings
- **Lifetime internal migrants:**
  - Provincia de Lima: 36.4% of the population are lifetime in-migrants (2017 census). — [INEI Nota de prensa 006-2021](https://www.inei.gob.pe/media/MenuRecursivo/noticias/nota-de-prensa-no-006-2021-inei.pdf)
  - Alternative calculation, Lima region incl. Lima Provincias and Callao: 2,985,643 people born in another department, about 31.6%. This is a fact-check of a claim that "80% are from the provinces", which is false. — [El Búho](https://elbuho.pe/?p=413014)
- **Recent migration flows:** Lima Metropolitana concentrates 40% of interdepartmental migration (554,000 people), according to BCRP *Moneda* 183. — [BCRP Moneda 183](https://www.bcrp.gob.pe/docs/Publicaciones/Revista-Moneda/moneda-183/moneda-183-09.pdf)
- **Fertility (ENDES 2024):**
  - TFR in Lima Metropolitana (43 districts) = 1.22 children per woman.
  - Departamento de Lima = 1.6.
  - Peru = 1.8 (INEI), or 1.76 (BCRP: lowest since 1986). — [INEI ENDES 2024 – Lima Metropolitana](https://proyectos.inei.gob.pe/endes/2024/departamentales/Endes15/pdf/Lima_Metropolitana.pdf); [BCRP Reporte de Inflación Mar 2026, recuadro 5](https://www.bcrp.gob.pe/docs/Publicaciones/Reporte-Inflacion/2026/marzo/reporte-de-inflacion-marzo-2026-recuadro-05.pdf)
- **Venezuelans – humanitarian/agency figures:**
  - More than 1,660,000 Venezuelans in Peru (data to June 2025), of whom close to 1,000,000 live in Lima. — [Corresponsables (R4V/GTRM-based)](https://www.corresponsables.com/per/actualidad/mas-de-163-000-personas-recibieron-apoyo-humanitario-en-el-peru-en-2025/)
  - A UNHCR Peru factsheet gives 1.54 million in Peru, over 1 million in Lima (date unclear). — [UNHCR data portal](https://data.unhcr.org/ar/documents/download/111158)
  - R4V/GTRM projected more than 1.7 million in Peru by Dec 2025. — [UNHCR Peru Strategy 2023–2026](https://www.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/2025-01/Peru%20-%20Strategy%202023%20%E2%80%93%202026_0.pdf)
- **Venezuelans – 2025 census:**
  - About 1 million foreigners were counted in Peru, 65.5% of them in Lima Metropolitana.
  - About 900,000 are Venezuelan. Whether that figure is national or Lima-only is unclear. — [Infobae, 10 Jul 2026](https://www.infobae.com/peru/2026/07/10/censo-nacional-2025-lima-metropolitana-concentra-el-mayor-crecimiento-poblacional/)
- **Venezuelans – ENPOVE 2024 (INEI/UNHCR/World Bank):** survey in 8 cities, Aug–Nov 2024. 61% have legal residence and about a third live in poverty. — [El Diario (VE), Dec 2025](https://eldiario.com/2025/12/19/venezolanos-en-peru-que-cuentan-residencia-vigente/)
- **Venezuelans – urban dimension:** Venezuelans in Lima are concentrated in the rental housing market. — [CIES 2022, "Dimensión urbana de la migración venezolana en Lima Metropolitana"](https://cies.org.pe/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/DIMENSION_Final.pdf); [Revista INVI 2024 (SciELO)](https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0718-83582024000200050)

### Inferences
- **Venezuelan figures do not agree.** Agency/R4V estimates (about 1 million in Lima, 1.6–1.7 million in Peru) are much higher than the census count (about 0.9 million Venezuelans; likely national). Possible reasons are undercounting of irregular migrants, onward/return migration, or different reference dates. Present both with their sources.
- With a TFR of 1.22, Lima's natural increase now depends mainly on its young age structure, which itself comes from earlier migration. Future growth will rely more on migration. This is an interpretation.

### Gaps
- No time series of migrant shares by census (1940–2017) was retrieved.
- No natural vs. migratory decomposition of growth for Lima.
- No official Migraciones (Superintendencia) figure for Lima.

## 5. Characteristic processes and spatial structures

### Takeaway
Lima's structure reflects several overlapping processes:
- informal land invasion and autoconstrucción, later titled by COFOPRI from 1996;
- growth along three "conos" (Norte, Este, Sur) that have consolidated into sub-centres;
- expansion onto desert hillsides (lomas);
- sharp segregation, made visible in the "Muro de la vergüenza" between La Molina/Surco and Villa María del Triunfo/San Juan de Miraflores;
- a dominant form of "subsequently enclosed neighbourhoods" (Plöger's "City of Cages").

### Cited Findings
- **Barriada share:** about 36.5% of Lima Metropolitana's population lived in more than 3,000 barriadas around 2010 (Matos Mar). — [Matos Mar, Desborde popular PDF](https://www.uv.mx/jose-marti/files/2018/08/Jose-Matos-Mar-Desborde-popular.pdf)
- **"Muro de la vergüenza":**
  - The wall mainly divides La Molina (Las Casuarinas) from Villa María del Triunfo. Some reports also locate segments between Santiago de Surco and San Juan de Miraflores; La República says it crosses five districts.
  - Length varies by source: about 4.5 km (Reuters) vs. about 9–10 km (Vatican News, Semana). Height is about 3 m.
  - The first section was built in 1985, reportedly justified by fear of Sendero Luminoso. Later extensions came in the 2000s (La Florida section about 2011) to prevent land invasions.
  - The Tribunal Constitucional ruled it violated freedom of movement and equality. Demolition began in Sept 2023. — [El Comercio](https://elcomercio.pe/mag/respuestas/que-es-exactamente-el-muro-de-la-verguenza-que-separa-la-molina-y-villa-maria-del-triunfo-lima-peru-nnda-nnlt-noticia/); [La República 2023](https://larepublica.pe/sociedad/2023/01/15/muro-de-la-verguenza-conoce-la-estructura-que-divide-a-surco-de-san-juan-de-miraflores-la-molina-villa-maria-del-triunfo-tribunal-constitucional-carlos-bruce); [La República 2022](https://larepublica.pe/sociedad/2022/12/29/muro-la-verguenza-la-historia-de-la-barrera-que-divide-la-molina-y-villa-maria-del-triunfo-y-por-que-genera-polemica-donde-queda-muro-de-la-verguenza-lima); [Vatican News, Sept 2023](https://www.vaticannews.va/es/mundo/news/2023-09/derriban-muro-de-la-verguenza-de-lima-despues-de-cuatro-decadas.html); [Semana](https://www.semana.com/mundo/articulo/el-muro-de-la-verguenza-en-lima-que-sera-destruido-tiene-10-kilometros-y-divide-a-poblacion-humilde-de-la-adinerada/202334/); [Reuters via Yahoo](https://es-us.noticias.yahoo.com/muro-verg%C3%BCenza-separa-ricos-pobres-160152963.html)
- **Gated communities (Plöger 2007):**
  - "Typical" gated communities are rare in Lima. The dominant type is the subsequently enclosed neighbourhood, with street gates and guards added later.
  - These enclaves have spread across most of the metropolitan area and cut across socio-economic lines.
  - Segregation patterns identified for Latin American cities until the 1980s "have remained largely intact" (citing Bähr & Mertins 1995). — [Plöger, Cybergeo 2007](https://journals.openedition.org/cybergeo/6785); [LSE eprint](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/3610)
  - Further commentary: [Equal Times](https://www.equaltimes.org/a-wall-separating-the-rich-from); [The Ecologist](https://theecologist.org/node/2987150)
- **Elite districts:** the ISS photo caption notes vegetated parks and golf courses in San Isidro and Miraflores. — [NASA ISS010-E-20111](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/earth-observations-taken-by-the-expedition-10-crew/7wHxCuceADbH6g?hl=en)
- **Recent peripheral expansion and risk:**
  - Sentinel-1 radar mapping of new informal urban clusters in Metropolitan Lima since 2017. — [Progress in Disaster Science 2024 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/9ecc471e13ab45b786a1e93e3c6152ab)
  - Exposure and vulnerability of recent informal areas. — [PUCP repository: Vulnerabilities and exposure of recent informal urban areas in Lima](https://repositorio.pucp.edu.pe/items/a0ef150b-56a2-472c-b8e7-382b164fd056); [NHESS 22, 65 (2022)](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/22/65/2022/nhess-22-65-2022.html)
  - A 2026 preprint estimates about 76 km² of urban growth in Metropolitan Lima over its study period, mostly peripheral. — [EGUsphere preprint 2025-5987](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2026/egusphere-2025-5987/egusphere-2025-5987.pdf)
- **Polycentricity:** PLANMET 2040 proposes new centralities concentrating jobs and services. The MML projects Lima Norte as a new economic pole by 2034, linked to the Chancay port. — [Gestión](https://gestion.pe/peru/mml-plan-de-desarrollo-urbano-2021-2040-propone-generar-nuevas-zonas-que-concentren-empleos-y-servicios-nndc-noticia/); [Gestión, Lima Norte](https://gestion.pe/economia/mml-proyecta-que-lima-norte-sea-el-nuevo-polo-economico-al-2034-pero-problemas-en-chancay-podrian-frenarlo-noticia/)
- **Zonal structure:** Ipsos's "6 caras de Lima Metropolitana 2025" profiles the zones (Lima Norte/Este/Sur/Centro/Moderna/Callao). — [Ipsos, Perfiles Zonales 2025](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/publication/documents/2025-09/Perfiles%20Zonales%202025_V3.pdf)
- **Historical structural analysis:** [CyTET 1990, "Estructura urbana metropolitana de Lima"](https://recyt.fecyt.es/index.php/CyTET/article/download/83711/61795/)

### Inferences
- **Applying the Latin American city model (Bähr/Mertins 1981/1995; Borsdorf 2003) to Lima.** These are interpretations for the poster:
  - (a) A colonial core (Cercado, damero grid, Plaza Mayor).
  - (b) An upper-class sector along Av. Arequipa/Leguía toward Miraflores, San Isidro, Barranco, then later La Molina and Surco.
  - (c) Industrial axes along Av. Argentina/Colonial toward Callao.
  - (d) A peripheral ring of barriadas/pueblos jóvenes in the conos, consolidating over time (e.g. Los Olivos becoming middle-class).
  - (e) Borsdorf's "fragmented city" phase: enclaves, malls (e.g. Mega Plaza in Lima Norte), the wall.
  - Plöger's finding that enclosure crosscuts social classes supports the fragmentation thesis.
- **The conos have grown into consolidated sub-centres.** SJL, SMP, Ate and Comas are now Peru's most populous districts (2025 census).

- No COFOPRI figure for Lima only.
- No source retrieved on central-city decline or renewal (Centro Histórico de Lima, UNESCO 1988/1991).
- No lomas-specific source retrieved.
